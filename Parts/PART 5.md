# Part V — Polymers and Composites

A milk bottle, a hip-joint liner, a gas pipe under a city street, a wind-turbine blade and an airliner wing skin share one thing: their load-bearing material is built from long organic chains. In the bottle, the pipe and the liner the chains are the whole story; in the blade and the wing they are the matrix that holds stiff glass or carbon fibres in place. Part V explains both. It starts from the molecule — a chain of thousands of repeat units — and shows how its length, architecture and the way it coils, tangles, crystallizes and vitrifies produce the behaviour engineers rely on and contend with: rubber that springs back because of entropy, plastics that soften at a glass transition, melts that thin when sheared, parts that creep, craze and crack. It then adds fibres, and asks how composite stiffness, strength and expansion follow from the constituents, how a single anisotropic ply behaves, and how stacks of plies are designed, damaged and manufactured.

## By the end of this part you should understand

- How step-growth and chain-growth polymerizations make chains, why the Carothers equation demands conversions above 99 %, and how catalysts control tacticity and branching.
- Why a polymer has a distribution of molecular weights, how its averages and dispersity are measured, and why toughness and melt flow depend on different averages.
- How random-walk statistics describe a chain, why rubber elasticity is entropic, and why most polymer pairs do not mix while block copolymers self-assemble.
- How polymers crystallize into lamellae and spherulites, and what free volume and the WLF equation say about the glass transition.
- Why melt viscosity scales as $M^{3.4}$, and how yielding, crazing, stress cracking and degradation limit polymer parts.
- How additives, vulcanization, recycling and biodegradable chemistries change properties and end of life.
- How the rule of mixtures, Halpin–Tsai and shear-lag models estimate composite properties, and when they fail.
- How ply stiffness transforms with angle, how laminate theory treats a stack of plies, and how failure criteria, delamination and impact damage set allowables.
- How composite parts are manufactured, and where metal- and ceramic-matrix composites take over.
